# TemanMu: 10-slide, five-minute pitch plan

Prepared 10 November 2026. This is a proposed structure, not an edited slide deck. Mentor comments below are paraphrased from the team's draft notes; recommendations are our interpretation.

## Central story

**TemanMu notices unusual household inactivity and coordinates a human check, helping older residents continue living independently.**

Follow one resident throughout: Madam Lee lives alone, has quiet days and values her independence. Her daughter cannot always be nearby. If her routine changes, who notices and who checks on her?

Keep the pitch on one pillar: safety through activity monitoring and human follow-up. Loneliness can explain Madam Lee's circumstances, but do not promise to solve loneliness through meter monitoring. Companion matching belongs in the appendix or future roadmap for this pitch.

Madam Lee is a demo persona, not a customer testimonial. Do not add invented interview quotations or validation results.

## Slide sequence and timing

## The 90-second demo recording

Use the existing simulator and actual caregiver/operator screens. Record short segments and edit them into one coherent Madam Lee story. The simulator supplies household events; the product value is the detection and follow-up visible in the apps.

| Video time | Recording | Spoken point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12 s | Normal Morning: water/appliance action and the resulting caregiver update. | “Her ordinary morning creates activity signals, without an app on her side.” |
| 12–25 s | Brief Going Out excerpt: card tap-out and outing context. | “A planned or usual outing should not be treated like inactivity at home.” |
| 25–43 s | A separate Unusual Inactivity scenario: home quiet beyond her learned limit. Make the change of scenario clear. | “Now consider a different day. The quiet period lasts longer than usual for her.” |
| 43–58 s | Simulated resident check-in call; no answer. | “TemanMu first tries to verify the concern with Madam Lee.” |
| 58–73 s | Matching concern on Sarah's caregiver screen and the township dashboard; responder selects “I'll Check Now”. | “An unresolved concern reaches the family and the designated local responder together.” |
| 73–85 s | Responder records a verified outcome and closes the case. | “The process records who checked and what happened.” |
| 85–90 s | Hold the completed incident and final status. | “The goal is a completed human check.” |

Recording requirements:

- Keep “Simulated household inputs and phone call; time compressed” visible and readable. The video is a demonstration, not evidence of real sensor detection or real telephone delivery.
- Explain the compressed clock once. Seconds of video do not represent a real-world alert-time commitment.
- Crop to the relevant UI. Use a single main view, or two enlarged views when showing matching notifications. Avoid three unreadable browser windows.
- Prepare each scenario from a clean reset and wait for its state to settle before recording. Trim waiting in the edit while retaining the time-compression label.
- Narrate live over the recording, or record a voiceover; avoid competing narration tracks.
- Keep a screenshot sequence as playback backup.
- Keep False Alarm Recovery as an optional Q&A clip. Do not add a fourth scenario to the main 90 seconds.
- Leave companion matching, settings tours, detailed home-entry policy and the separate landed UI demo out of this recording.

## What we can accurately say about ML today

Current repository evidence is newer than the general product-context document's statement that routine learning is planned.

- `backend/ml/README.md` documents two implemented training pipelines, trained on eight weeks of simulated history: six weeks for training and two for testing.
- The **Quiet Gap Model** uses gradient-boosting quantile regression to learn gaps between water uses by time of day and day of week.
- The **Outing Model** uses gradient-boosting quantile regression to learn usual outing durations, with a calibrated allowance before a concern is raised.
- Learned weekly tables are exported into `shared/ml/A-105_quiet_gap.json` and `shared/ml/A-105_outing.json`.
- `backend/app/learned_baseline.py` reads the relevant scene-time limits on demo reset and scales them into demo seconds. The runtime rules engine uses these limits; it is not continuously training a model as the demo runs.
- The current implementation is **not the earlier proposed Isolation Forest approach**. Present the model that is now in the repository.
- These models estimate routine timing. They do not identify a fall, diagnose illness, understand every activity or establish that a resident is safe.
- Household electricity changes add context. A fan, refrigerator or air-conditioner continuing to run is not proof of fresh resident activity.

Suggested 25-second explanation:

> “TemanMu learns two parts of her routine: how long she is normally quiet at home, and how long she is normally out. It uses the time of day and day of the week to set personal limits. If activity stays unusual beyond those limits, it starts a check-in. Our prototype learns from simulated history; a pilot will test it in real homes.”

Do not put algorithm parameters, training code or unverified accuracy claims on the main slide. Show one clear routine example; keep technical detail in reserve.

## Landed homes and responsibility

Use service coverage, rather than property title, to explain the operating model:

| Setting | Proposed response arrangement | What must be clear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apartment with contracted building response | Authorised building/security team accepts checks. | Participation, availability, scope, access and escalation must be agreed. |
| Landed home inside an agreed township service area | Designated township team or authorised contracted responder. | Do not assume every guard or Auxiliary Police team has accepted welfare-check duties. |
| Individual landed home without a guard or response service | Family plus an explicitly nominated nearby contact or contracted service, where available. | Do not advertise guaranteed nearby attendance until someone has agreed to provide it. |

Proposed division of roles: TemanMu provides monitoring and coordination; the contracted service operator manages response coverage; the designated responder accepts and performs an authorised check; family participates in verification; a person decides when emergency services are needed. Written agreements and qualified review must determine legal responsibilities before a real service launches.

The current `/township-landed` view is a separate UI-only illustration. It does not send notifications, dispatch people or save cases to the backend. Use it to explain the intended deployment, not as proof of integrated landed operations.

## Business explanation for a short pitch

Position the opportunity as an extension of township living and family reassurance. Sime Darby Property's role is a proposed sponsor, site partner or service buyer; do not assume it is entering clinical elderly care.

Suggested business sentence:

> “We propose installation plus a recurring fee per enrolled home, funded initially through a township pilot. That fee must cover connectivity, platform support, maintenance and agreed response provision. The pilot will test what families and operators are willing to pay.”

A CSR-funded pilot can support access and learning. It does not establish recurring commercial demand. Any later family subscription remains a model to test.

Keep the following in the appendix if numbers are available:

- One-off costs: equipment, electrical work, plumbing, commissioning and onboarding.
- Recurring costs: data, hosting, support, maintenance and response staffing/provision.
- Revenue logic: enrolled homes × monthly fee, with installation charged separately or explicitly amortised.
- Viability test: fees cover those costs at a realistic enrolment level. Include both false-alert workload and response travel time.
- Evidence to obtain: household eligibility within the proposed site, opt-in interest, willingness to pay, supplier quotations and operator capacity.

National prevalence establishes a need; it is not a count of paying customers or evidence of demand inside a particular Sime Darby township. Do not invent a price to fill the slide.

## What to move out of the main ten slides

Separate team biographies, companion matching, full technology stack, algorithm details, supplier catalogues, detailed cost model, training metrics, privacy/access-policy text and additional scenario demos belong in Q&A material. Keep only the points needed to understand the problem, see the system work, and assess the proposed pilot.

## Completion checks for the eventual deck

- Exactly ten main slides, including opening and closing.
- One safety problem carried through the persona, evidence, solution and outcome.
- “No app for the elderly resident” visible on slide 4 and demonstrated on slide 6.
- Three signal types described accurately; access-card integration is conditional.
- Simulated training, household inputs, phone delivery and time compression labelled accurately.
- No-guard landed case answered without assuming a responder exists.
- Buyer, revenue structure and principal costs briefly explained.
- Hardware, minute-level data and partnerships labelled as pilot proposals.
- Closing explains a small supervised pilot rather than making a funding or partnership ask.
- Rehearsed with the embedded video to stay within five minutes.
